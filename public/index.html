Intersecting Parallels

Where you stand and what you see. Set vanishing points, and every line you draw runs to one of them — so when you move a point, the drawing follows.

Three things to try

  • Draw — drag on the canvas. The line takes the nearest guide: a vanishing point, vertical, horizontal, or 45° if you turn it on. Swing toward another guide mid-stroke and it changes its mind.
  • Box — two steps, and the second one comes to you. The first drag sets the height and the depth toward whichever point you drag toward; then the other depth is immediately live, so just drag again anywhere. Every corner stays draggable afterwards.
  • Place — drag a vanishing point and watch everything bound to it move. That is the whole idea of the app in one gesture.

Everything stays on your device. No account, no network — it works with the aeroplane mode on. Every corner can be dragged, nudged with the arrow keys once selected, or typed exactly in the Points panel. Drawing a line or a box still needs a drag — that one is on the list.

Intersecting Parallels — where you stand and what you see.

What this is

A drawing tool for perspective construction. You put vanishing points on the page, and every line you draw is bound to one of them — so when you move a point, the whole drawing re-solves and follows it. That is the difference between a construction and a set of strokes that merely look right.

  • Free, and yours to keep — no account, no sign-up, no trial.
  • Everything stays on this device. Nothing is uploaded, and there is no analytics of any kind.
  • Works with the aeroplane mode on, once it has loaded the first time.
  • Export the outline as SVG or PNG and finish it wherever you like.

What this is not

  • Not a paint app. No brushes, no pressure, no opacity, no colour. It builds the geometry and hands it over.
  • Not a 3D modeller. There is no camera you fly around and no mesh underneath. A box here is a flat drawing that obeys perspective rules, not a solid the app could rotate for you.
  • Not curvilinear. Straight-line perspective only — fisheye and panoramic projections bow lines that this app keeps straight.
  • Not a tracer. It will show a photograph underneath so you can draw over it, but it will not find the lines in it for you.
  • Drawing still needs a drag. Every corner can be typed or nudged with the arrow keys, but starting a line or a box needs a pointer. That one is on the list of what is still broken.

Put it on your home screen

It then opens like an app, full screen, with no browser bars — and works offline.

  • iPhone and iPad (Safari) — the Share button (a square with an arrow out of the top), then Add to Home Screen.
  • iPhone and iPad (Chrome) — the Share button, then Add to Home Screen. Chrome on iOS uses Safari underneath.
  • Android (Chrome) — the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app, depending on your version.
  • Android (Firefox) — the ⋮ menu, then Install.
  • Windows, Mac and Linux (Chrome or Edge) — the install icon at the right-hand end of the address bar, or the ⋮ / … menu, then Install.
  • Firefox on the desktop — no install; bookmark it. It still works offline.

Keyboard

  • Arrow keys nudge a focused vanishing point; Shift makes larger steps.
  • Every point has exact x and y boxes in the Points panel.
  • Undo and redo are in the toolbar, one step per gesture.

Where the drawing comes from

Nowhere but you. This app has no feeds, no datasets, no maps and no external service of any kind, so there is nothing here to credit or attribute. What it holds is:

  • Your drawings, in this browser's own storage on this device. Clearing the browser's site data deletes them.
  • A reference photograph, if you load one. It is read from your device and stored beside the drawing. It is never uploaded, and the app cannot see it once you remove it.
  • Your settings. That is the whole list.

The typefaces are whatever your own device provides — none are downloaded.

If something is wrong

Report a problem at the bottom of this panel writes out everything the app knows about your drawing as plain text, and copies it. Send that rather than a screenshot — a picture cannot show why a corner would not go where you put it, and that is usually the part that matters. Read it first if you like; it holds no account and no location.

Send it to the issue tracker on GitHub, or to whoever gave you this app.

Accessibility

Everything on the canvas is reachable from the panel, because a canvas cannot be tabbed into. Contrast, target sizes and keyboard routes are checked by a gate that blocks every release, across eight screens, two themes and two phone widths. If something here is hard to use, that is a bug worth reporting.
